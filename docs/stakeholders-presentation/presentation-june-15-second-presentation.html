SP Resolution — the process we've modeled
8 / 16
SP Resolution — key design points

Preliminary vs. final series number

Preliminary number at first intake. Final official number only after Mayor's signature at docketing. These are distinct events — discussed in the questions section.

Third reading: final vote only

The system enforces that Third Reading is a vote on the final version. No debates; only minor formal amendments are accepted before the vote is called.

Committee referral to two committees

Most measures go to the subject-matter committee and the Committee on Laws simultaneously. The system assigns both at the same time.

Mayor's veto and override

The Mayor may veto. The system records the veto and opens the override step, requiring 8 of 12 votes. The override outcome is also recorded.

Certified urgent path

When declared certified urgent, a measure skips committee referral and goes directly to Second Reading. Guidance needed on who can authorize — covered in questions.

Panlalawigan transmission

After the Mayor signs, the Secretariat transmits. The 30-day review clock starts from the transmission date, tracked automatically.

Panlalawigan review — how the system handles each outcome

Valid

Fully approved. System records the Panlalawigan's resolution number and date, notifies relevant offices (CPDO, Budget Office, etc.). Document archived and published.

Valid-in-part

Some provisions inconsistent with law. Document placed in a special queue. SP Secretary selects one of three actions: resolve as-is, refer to City Legal Office, or route back to committee.

Returned

Document disapproved. System raises a high-priority alert for the SP Secretary, City Legal Office, and Mayor's Office, requiring immediate coordinated action. Status clearly flagged.

Referred to committee (Panlalawigan's own)

The 30-day provincial clock continues running. The system tracks and notifies the SP Secretary as the deadline approaches.

No Panlalawigan action for 30 days

On Day 31, system automatically transitions to "Deemed Approved" per Section 56(d) of RA 7160. SP Secretary receives immediate notification. Legal basis recorded automatically.

Operative in its entirety (Appropriation Ordinances)

Treated the same as Valid. Outcome recorded with the Panlalawigan's resolution number.

Two committees at once — how we're designing this
From the Notice of Committee Hearing logs, the vast majority of measures go to two committees simultaneously. This is standard — not a special case.

How the system handles this

At the referral step, both committees are assigned simultaneously. Each schedules its hearing and submits its report through the system. Once all assigned committees have submitted, the matter is ready to advance to Second Reading. The SP Secretary confirms readiness. A Notice of Committee Hearing is generated for each assigned committee.

Acting officials — a first-class feature (1 of 2)
From the Designation logs, more than ten Vice Mayor-as-Acting-Mayor designations appeared in 2023–2024. This is routine and high-frequency. The system treats it accordingly.

When a Designation is logged

The system creates an authority record with a specific start date and end date. All documents that would normally go to the Mayor are automatically re-routed to the Acting Mayor for the duration of the designation.

During the delegation period

Actions taken by the Acting Mayor are recorded with their name and the capacity they acted in: "Acting Mayor — per Designation D 2026-01." The original authority and acting person are always clearly identified in every record.

When the end date arrives

The delegation expires automatically. No manual cleanup or reassignment is needed. All routing returns to the original official.

Confirming each delegation

The Platform Administrator reviews and formally confirms the scope of every delegation before it takes effect. Logging the document alone does not activate the delegation — confirmation is required.
