Q-03 — Does the 10-day lapse rule apply to resolutions?
Confirmed — Ordinances

After the Vice Mayor signs, the Mayor has 10 calendar days to sign, veto, or allow the measure to lapse into law. Inaction for 10 days causes automatic effect.

Open — Resolutions

The flowchart confirms the Mayor's signature is required and the Mayor can veto resolutions — but it is unclear whether the same 10-day lapse rule applies.

Questions for the secretariat

  • Does the 10-day lapse rule apply to SP Resolutions the same way it does to Ordinances?
  • If the Mayor vetoes a resolution, does the SP hold an override vote using the same 2/3 threshold (8 of 12 members)?
  • Is there a formal transmittal document sent from the SP Secretariat to the Mayor's Office for each resolution?

Q-04 — Multi-committee referral   ·   Q-05 — Certified urgent
Q-04

Multi-committee referral: workflow design

  • When two committees are assigned, does the SP wait for both reports before Second Reading, or only for the primary committee's report?
  • If both are required and one committee is slower — does the SP Secretary follow up directly, or does the step auto-escalate?
  • Is the Committee on Laws always co-referred, or are there document types that bypass it?
  • For a multi-committee hearing, is a single NCH issued for all committees, or a separate NCH per committee?
Q-05

Certified urgent: authorization rules

  • Who has authority to declare a measure certified urgent — the Mayor, the Vice Mayor, a majority of the SP, or another authority?
  • Is the declaration made in writing (formal certification from the Mayor), or verbally declared during First Reading?
  • Can any document type be certified urgent, or only certain types?
  • How often is this path used in practice — roughly how many measures per year?
  • If certified urgent, is it debated and voted in the same session, or is a 24-hour notice period still required?
