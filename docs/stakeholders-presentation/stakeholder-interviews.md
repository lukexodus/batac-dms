## **General Stakeholder Interviews**

### **Process Discovery**

* How does a document move from creation to archival within the LGU? Are there any established documentation for the process flow of each document type?  
  * Are there formal SOPs or informal practices?  
    * Are workflows standardized across offices, or does each office follow its own process?  
  * What are all the document types you regularly handle?  
  * Who touches the documents at each stage?  
    * What happens when the assigned person is absent, on leave, or unavailable?  
      * Is there a formally designated alternate, or is it handled informally?  
      * Does absence pause the document, or does it automatically route to someone else?  
  * What are the possible state changes a document can go through?  
    * Can a document be cancelled after it has started routing? By whom? Is a reason required?  
    * Can it be returned for revision? By whom? To whom does it return?  
    * How long should each step ideally take?  
    * What happens when a step exceeds that time? Who is notified, and by what means?  
    * Are there documents that can skip steps under certain conditions?  
  * What is the formal organizational structure — offices, departments, and reporting lines?  
    * Is there an official organizational chart we can reference or obtain a copy of?  
    * Are there any offices or positions not on the chart that still handle documents?

### **Current Pain Points**

* What are the most common problems with the current paper-based process?  
  * What documents are most frequently lost, misplaced, or significantly delayed?  
  * What information do you most frequently need but cannot quickly locate?  
  * What tasks consume the most manual effort that a digital system should eliminate?  
* How do citizens currently check the status of their requests or complaints?  
  * Is the status recorded at all stages, or only at receipt and completion?  
* What is the realistic current processing time for \[specific document type\]?  
  * Which office holds documents the longest?  
  * What is the primary cause of delays?  
* Is there a pattern of processing documents outside official channels — by phone, chat, or informal routing — to avoid paperwork? If so, for which document types?

### **Access and Classification**

* Who is allowed to access each document type, and to what degree?  
  * Are there documents that are confidential even from some internal employees or other departments?  
  * Are there documents that must be visible to multiple offices simultaneously?  
  * Should citizens be able to track their own documents in progress without visiting City Hall?  
* How are documents currently accessed?  
  * Physical copy only  
  * Digital copy — if so, through what means: shared drives, email, or an existing system?  
* Who has authority to define or change who may access a given document type?  
* What is the current practice when a confidential document is requested by an unauthorized party?

### **Security**

* What security measures would you require for the system?  
  * Authentication model  
    * Should each person have an individual account, or are shared office accounts acceptable?  
    * Is a login required once per session, or before each significant action?  
  * Current security posture  
    * Are any existing digital systems protected by multi-factor authentication?  
    * Have there been any security incidents — unauthorized access or data leaks — in recent years?  
  * Account management  
    * Should accounts be created by staff themselves, or provisioned by IT and handed off with a temporary password?  
  * Budget and appetite for advanced security measures  
    * Multi-factor authentication (e.g., one-time codes sent to a phone)  
    * Physical access cards or tokens  
    * Biometrics  
  * Should every action on every document be logged in an audit trail? Who should be able to view those logs?

### **Integration and Data Migration**

* What existing software systems does the LGU currently use?  
  * Are any of these systems still actively used and must continue to operate alongside the new platform?  
  * Can we obtain documentation or process flows for any existing systems?  
  * What hardware and equipment is currently in use across offices?  
* Does any existing system hold data that the new platform needs to import?  
  * Are there historical documents that need to be migrated into the new system?  
    * Approximately how many documents, and how far back do the records go?  
    * What format are they in — physical paper, scanned PDFs, digital files, or a mix?  
    * Is there any structured metadata attached to historical records (document type, date, author, office), or is that information embedded only in the physical document itself?  
  * Are there reports currently produced manually in Excel or other tools that should be automated?  
* Does the LGU have an existing .gov.ph domain or official government web presence?  
  * Has coordination with DICT been initiated for a .gov.ph domain for the new system?  
* Are there any known DICT e-government standards or interoperability requirements that the system must comply with?

### **Legal and Compliance**

* Are there DILG, COA, CSC, or other regulatory requirements that affect how documents are managed or retained?  
  * What are the legally mandated retention periods for each document type?  
  * Are there COA circulars that specifically apply to Batac City's records?  
  * Are there applicable DILG circulars on records management or digital government systems?  
  * Does the National Archives Act (RA 10095\) apply to LGU records? Has the National Archives of the Philippines been consulted on this?  
* Has the LGU designated a Data Protection Officer under RA 10173?  
  * If yes, who holds that designation and in which office?  
  * Has a Privacy Impact Assessment been conducted or planned for the new system?  
* What are the ARTA-mandated processing timeframes for each service the LGU provides?  
  * Which document types does the LGU classify as simple, complex, or highly technical under ARTA?  
* After a physical document is digitized, must the physical original still be retained?  
  * For how long, and under what conditions?  
  * Has the Commission on Audit confirmed — or been asked — whether digital records are accepted as equivalent to physical originals for audit purposes?  
* Is the City Legal Office aware of the Electronic Commerce Act (RA 8792\) and its implications for digital records and electronic signatures? Has the City Legal Office reviewed whether digital approvals have legal standing?  
* Are there any documents currently under a legal hold — meaning they cannot be disposed of regardless of their retention schedule?  
  * Who has authority to place a document under legal hold, and who must authorize lifting it?

### **Technical Environment**

* What devices do employees use — desktop, laptop, or mobile?  
  * What is the primary device on which the system will be used per office?  
  * Will the system need to function on personal mobile phones?  
* What are the network specifications at each office location?  
  * What ISP and plan are they subscribed to?  
  * Are all offices connected to a single LAN, or do they each connect independently via the internet?  
  * How reliable and fast is the connection at each location?  
  * Are there locations with intermittent or unreliable connectivity?  
* Is there backup power — UPS or generator — at critical locations?  
  * What is the uptime and load capacity of each backup power system?  
* Who will maintain the system after it is deployed?  
  * Does the IT team have experience managing containerized applications or cloud-hosted systems?  
  * What is the IT team's current capacity — how many staff, and what are their primary responsibilities?

### **Success Criteria**

* How will you know the system is a success after 6 months of use?  
  * What are the ultimate outcomes the LGU expects to see?  
  * Which single feature would most immediately reduce your daily workload?  
* What would constitute a failure of this project?  
  * What are the critical risks or red lines that cannot be crossed?  
* What were the criteria for any previous system or process that was replaced or evaluated?  
* If staff continue processing documents outside the system — by phone, messenger, or physical routing — after the system is deployed, how should that be handled? Is there organizational will to enforce the system as the official channel?

  * Who prepares and approves the session call?

  * What specifically differs between a resolution and an ordinance in terms of steps required?  
  * Are there resolutions that do not require committee referral? How is that determined?  
* How are committee assignments made?

  * Is there a quorum requirement, and what happens if quorum is not met?  
* What is the Liga ng mga Barangay representative's role in SP sessions?  
  * Do Liga resolutions follow the same routing process as SP resolutions, or a different one?  
* How are SP session minutes prepared, reviewed, approved, and archived?  
  * Who drafts the minutes — the SP Secretary or a designated staff member?  
  * Are session minutes treated as a standalone document type, or as a sub-document attached to the session record?  
  * Who must sign off on the minutes before they are considered official?  
* Who has final authority to release approved resolutions and ordinances?  
* What information do you need to see before calling a document for a vote?
